|---|---|---|---|

| fg_make rim | `winner_FGA_rim` (lgbm) | 309,000 | ~0.33 |
| fg_make jump2 | `winner_FGA_jump2` (lgbm) | 407,000 | ~0.23 |
| fg_make FGA_3 | `winner_FGA_3` (A_team ridge) | 5,500,000 | ~0.25 |

**The clock arm is about 85% of the engine's model cost.** Nine LightGBM quantile
regressions, once per possession; everything else together is under a sixth of
it.

End-to-end, single process, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`:

| batch | possessions | wall | poss/s/core | machine state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 120 sims (40 games x 3 seeds) | 17,406 | 25.2 s | 691 | lightly contended |
| **6,000 sims (300 games x 20 seeds)** | **862,621** | **1,000.9 s** | **862** | heavily contended (14 engine workers + other agents' jobs) |

**The measured production-batch rate is 862 possessions/s/core, not the ~3,500
the per-model benchmarks projected.** The projection (the clock arm's own 4,900
rows/s/core less what everything else costs) was taken on a near-idle core; the
862 was taken with the box saturated. The honest statement is that the true
dedicated-core number lies between them and **has not been measured**, because no
point in this session had free cores.

**Against the target** (5,700 games x 200 seeds in under 2 hours on 20 cores):
the season is 5,710 x 200 x ~145 = **1.66e8 possessions**.

- At the measured 862 poss/s/core: 1.93e5 core-seconds = **2.7 hours on 20
  cores -- the target is MISSED by ~35%.**
- At the clock arm's own isolated 4,900 rows/s/core, which is a *lower bound on
  cost* since it ignores every other model and all state bookkeeping: 3.4e4
  core-seconds = 28 minutes. So the target is only reachable if a dedicated box
  recovers most of the 5.7x gap between the two measurements.

**Conclusion: on the evidence actually collected, the batched-predict approach
does NOT hit the target, and the lookup-table export is required work, not a
contingency.** The deliverable's own escape clause therefore applies, and the
binning error it demands be reported has **not** been measured -- see below.

The stated fallback if it misses is the lookup export. Its cheapest form is
already on disk and wired: the clock bake-off's
`reference_not_adopted_empirical.pkl` IS a lookup table
(`EmpiricalArm.level_pmfs`, a (n_cells, 91) pmf over 7 nested binned state
dimensions of sizes 6 x 5 x 2 x 3 x 3 x 5 x 2), available behind
`ENGINE_CLOCK=reference_empirical`. Binning the quantile arm itself has **not**
been done and its binning error has **not** been measured.

## 3. What the engine actually produced, and the component diagnosis

From a **6,000-simulation development sample** (300 randomly chosen games x 20
seeds, 862,621 possessions). This is NOT a gate read -- it is 300 of 5,710 games,
graded by hand against the season reference rather than by `eval_gates.py`, with
no seed-count study behind it -- but at this size the descriptive means are
informative. (A 120-simulation smoke sample taken earlier agrees on every sign.)

| quantity | engine v0 | actual 2025 | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| total points | 143.74 | 145.5 | -1.8 |
| possessions/game | 71.89 | 67.875 | **+4.01** |
| points per possession | 1.000 | 1.071 | **-0.071** |
| 3PA share of FGA | 0.387 | 0.392 | -0.005 |
| FTA/FGA | 0.332 | 0.332 | **0.000** |
| OREB% | 0.295 | 0.296 | -0.001 |
| rim share of FGA | 0.366 | -- | -- |
| TOV per possession | 0.171 | -- | -- |
| home margin | +3.85 | +5.74 (all games) | -1.9 (300-game subset, noisy) |
| OT rate | 1.3% | 5.58% | **-4.3 pp** |
| minutes per team-game (named players) | 197.3 of 200 | 200 | -2.7 (anonymous tail slots) |
| players with > 0 minutes per team-game (named) | 8.81 | ~9-10 | low |

Read the shape of that table carefully, because it is the whole diagnosis in one
place: **every per-possession rate the L3 sub-models own lands within half a
percentage point of actual** -- 3PA share -0.005, FTA/FGA exactly 0.000, OREB%
-0.001 -- while **the possession count is +4.01 and points per possession is
-0.071**. The sub-models are producing the right mix per possession; the engine is
giving them four too many possessions to do it on.

### Diagnosis, in order of size

**1. The clock model (G1, and through it G2 and G9).** The engine puts 4.01 too
many possessions in a game and scores 6.6% too little on each of them, and the
two very nearly cancel in the game total (-1.8 of 145.5, i.e. -1.2%). That is the
offsetting-error signature CLAUDE.md's multi-level-evidence rule exists to catch:
a nearly-passing total over a broken denominator. The component is not in doubt --
the clock bake-off
adopted **nothing** specifically because no arm passed its own emergent G1 gate
(`docs/models/clock/verdict.json`: "no (arm, feature set) combination passed the
pre-registered emergent G1 and PIT gates on F2"), and Decision 7 records this as
the acknowledged risk of making pace emergent: *"G1 now depends on the
clock-consumption model being right by state."* The engine inherits that failure
exactly as predicted. Round 2 of the clock bake-off was still running while this
engine was built.

Note what this is *not*: it is not a points-per-possession defect in the shot
models. 3PA share misses by 0.005, FTA/FGA by 0.000 and OREB% by 0.001 -- the
possession-outcome, free-throw and rebound models are each landing their own rate
essentially exactly. The PPP miss is the denominator.

**2. The overtime rate (G7).** 1.3% against 5.58%. The engine reaches the end of
regulation tied far too rarely. That is a **dispersion** symptom in the late
second half, not an overtime-rule symptom -- the OT rules themselves are
implemented and tested (5-minute periods, fresh jump ball, team fouls carried
over as an extension of the second half, repeat until untied, and
`tests/test_engine.py` asserts no game ends tied). Control fails the same gate
from the opposite direction (1.83%) with a completely different mechanism (a
Gaussian margin twice too wide, yet too few exact ties), which says the shared
cause is that neither engine reproduces the *discreteness* of a basketball
margin near zero. For the possession engine the suspects are the clock model's
late-period duration distribution and the engine's end-of-period censoring read
(model.md section 2.2), in that order. The 6,000-simulation sample also shows
12,082 possessions censored by the period end out of 862,621 (1.4%, i.e. about
two per game, the expected two period boundaries), so the censoring read is
behaving as designed and is the second suspect, not the first.

**3. The team-foul / personal-foul split.** Team fouls (which drive the bonus)
come from the foul events the L3 vocabulary can express plus a measured
non-shooting-foul accrual rate of 0.12335 per possession; personal fouls (which
drive foul-outs and therefore minutes) come from `rotation`'s fitted `fpm`
hazard. The two are **not reconciled**, and the engine says so
(`provisional_foul_accrual`). On the 6,000-simulation sample FTA/FGA lands at
0.332 against an actual 0.332, so the accrual rate is not visibly mis-set at the
aggregate -- but that is a ratio, and it is being hit while the possession count
is 4.01 too high, so the FTA *count* per game is correspondingly inflated. The
rate cannot be validated independently until the clock is fixed. 219 of 862,621
possessions awarded a bonus trip while not in the bonus (0.025%), counted and not
suppressed.

**4. FGA_3 and Decision 8.** `winner_FGA_3.joblib` predates Decision 8 and holds
`team_baseline`, whose shooter slope ratio is 0.0086 -- flat in shooter skill,
which Decision 8 calls out explicitly. The engine defaults to
`ENGINE_FG3=decision8` and runs its own LightGBM refit for that class, recorded
in `run_meta.json`. This is a sub-model export defect, not an engine defect, and
it is the one place the engine deliberately does not use an on-disk "winner".

**5. The player layer (G8).** 8.81 named players with minutes per team-game
against roughly 9-10 real, and 197.3 of 200 team minutes reaching named players.
Both have the same cause: 10.0% of team-games have no usable rotation prior (a
team's first game of the season) and fall back to anonymous fitted tail slots,
which carry minutes but no athlete identity and are excluded from
`players.parquet`. Separately, L19's open defect (every allocator
under-concentrates the top of the lineup) is inherited unchanged and will show in
G8's top-1 FGA share.

## 5. The honest bottom line

ENGINE v0 is wired end to end and produces contract-valid output with a real
player layer, which Control cannot. It does **not** yet match Control on the
game-level gates, and the reason is a single identified component rather than a
diffuse inaccuracy: the clock model, whose own bake-off adopted nothing on the
gate the engine now fails. Per the standing rule "never accept a downstream stage
that compensates for a known upstream bias", no adjustment was applied anywhere,
and none should be until the clock round-2 winner is wired and G1 is re-read.

## A3. THE FIRST-ORDER DEFECT: `score_diff` closes a feedback loop

G5 exposed it. The engine's per-team points SD is 19.97 against a real ~11, its
margin SD across simulations is 34.99, and its home/away score correlation is
**-0.615** where reality is +0.253 -- within one matchup the TOTAL is stable
while the SPLIT swings wildly. Possessions are not the cause: the two sides'
possession counts differ by an SD of only 1.34 and correlate at 0.96, so the
ball alternates correctly.

An ablation isolates it. 60 games x 20 seeds, paired streams, the `score_diff`
and `score_diff x seconds_remaining` state columns redirected to an always-zero
column for ONE adapter at a time. The harness reproduces the baseline row
exactly, which is its own validity check.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **baseline** | 34.61 | 16.36 | 19.77 | **-0.6363** | 33.79 | 71.61 | 145.65 |
| clock `score_diff` off | 33.01 | 14.55 | 18.37 | -0.6750 | 32.25 | **67.84** | 136.69 |
| event `score_diff` off | 39.36 | 16.46 | 22.09 | -0.7041 | 38.52 | 71.48 | 146.14 |

| clock+event off | 37.88 | 14.93 | 20.60 | -0.7312 | 37.23 | 67.71 | 136.98 |

| **actual 2025** | **14.63** | **18.95** | ~11 | **+0.2532** | -- | **67.88** | **145.51** |

### Two separate defects, two separate owners

1. **`fg_make` owns the dispersion blow-up.** Removing `score_diff` from the
   three shot-make models alone takes margin SD from 34.61 to 11.35, per-team
   points SD from 19.77 to 10.65, and the home/away correlation from -0.64 to
   +0.42. Nothing else comes close; removing it from the event model makes
   things *worse*, so the event layer is damping the loop, not driving it.
2. **`clock` owns the possession inflation.** Removing `score_diff` from the
   clock alone takes possessions/game from 71.61 to **67.84** against an actual
   67.88 -- i.e. G1's entire +4.24 miss is this one feature, not the clock arm's
   general quality. This revises section 7 item 1 above and RESUME.md's "first
   thing to fix": the clock bake-off's L20 horn-truncation root cause is real,
   but the engine's G1 miss has a second, larger, and different cause.

### Why this happens, stated as a modelling claim

`score_diff` is a **consequence** of the outcome being simulated, used as a
**driver** of it. In training it is exogenous and carries team-quality
information -- a team that is ahead is ahead partly because it is better -- so a
shot-make model that sees it learns a coefficient that is partly a selection
effect. In simulation that coefficient closes a loop: a random early lead is
read by `fg_make` as evidence of a strong offence, which then shoots better,
which widens the lead. Team strength is **already** carried by the ratings and
shooter features, so the engine double-counts it, with feedback. The signature
is exactly the table above: margin variance inflated ~3x, total variance roughly
preserved, and the two teams' scores driven apart.

### What was NOT done

No coefficient shrinkage, no margin clipping, no variance rescaling, no
recalibration (`CLAUDE.md`, "no hand tuning on engine output"). The ablation is a
**diagnostic that names the responsible sub-model**, not a fix -- and the "all
off" row shows why deletion is not the fix either: it costs 12 points of game
total (133.24 against an actual 145.51), because the state block genuinely
carries real signal (it cleared its own noise floor by 30-140x at L3).

**What needs pre-registering**, for the PM: a bake-off over parametrisations of
game state that cannot re-encode team strength -- `score_diff` residualised
against the pregame rating difference and elapsed fraction; a "surprise" term
(`score_diff` minus its expectation given the matchup and the time remaining);
or dropping it from the scoring-stage models while keeping it in the clock. This
blocks G5, G7 and every market number, and it is a larger effect than anything
currently in the clock round-3 queue.

### The lookup table buys 1.27x, not the 5x the plan assumed

This corrects section 2 and section 6 of `model.md`. The claim that "the clock
arm is ~85% of the engine's model cost" came from isolated per-model benchmarks.
**In the real loop the cost is dominated by state bookkeeping, design-matrix
assembly and the other six adapters**, so replacing the clock's boosters with a
pure table gains only 27%. At 1.65e8 possessions for 5,710 games x 200 seeds
that is 52.3 core-hours live against 41.1 binned -- **2.6 h against 2.05 h on 20
cores**. The lookup export therefore only just reaches the 2-hour target, and no
further optimisation of the clock can help, because the clock is no longer where
the time goes.

### `ENGINE_CLOCK=reference_empirical` did not previously work

Section 6 above says the empirical arm "is wired". It was not: it failed at
adapter load. Two reasons, both now fixed:

1. It declares `chance_number_at_start`, which the engine's state block did not
   carry, and `plan_features` correctly refuses to hand a model a column it
   never saw. The feature is identically **1.0** at a possession's start
   (`clock.build_design` sets a literal 1.0; every arm drops it as
   zero-variance), and the engine now supplies that same constant rather than
   the live chance number, because the clock is drawn once per POSSESSION.
2. `EmpiricalArm._codes` reads two RAW columns that are not in the arm's own
   declared feature list -- `prev_end` as a string level and `season` as an
   integer -- so a matrix built from `arm.features` could not drive it.
   `ClockAdapter` now rebuilds both: `prev_end` is decoded from the five dummies
   the plan already carries (`clock.PREV_END_LEVELS` and the engine's
   `state.PREV_END_CODE` are built from the same tuple in the same order, and an
   all-zero dummy row is `period_start`), and `season` is passed at load.

## A6. Honest bottom line, revised

The engine is wired end to end, tested (13/13 in three flag configurations),
produces contract-valid output, and now runs the **adopted** possession-outcome
winners on a leak-checked monthly schedule with `provisional_event=False`. Its
per-possession event mix is right to within 1.4 pp and its total bias is better
than the close.

It fails the gates, and this session replaced a one-line diagnosis ("the clock")
with two specific, separately-owned, separately-fixable defects:
**`fg_make`'s `score_diff` closes a feedback loop that triples margin variance**,
and **the clock's `score_diff` accounts for the entire +4.24 possession miss**.
Neither was corrected anywhere. Both need their own pre-registration, and the
first is larger than anything currently queued.
