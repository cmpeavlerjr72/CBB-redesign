# ENGINE v0 -- F2 (season 2025) gate read, side by side with the Control

Generated 2026-09-10 at a hard session stop. Engine spec:
`docs/models/engine/model.md`. Unfinished work and the resume commands:
`docs/models/engine/RESUME.md`. Control column copied verbatim from
`docs/tests/gates_control_F2_A_own_2026-09-10.md` and
`docs/tests/market_games_control_F2_A_own_2026-09-10.md`.

**Nothing in the engine was adjusted to move a gate.** CLAUDE.md "no hand tuning
on engine output": every miss below is reported with the sub-model diagnosed, and
the first response is "which sub-model produces the wrong distribution", never
"what adjustment closes the gap".

   in blocks of 250 games x 25 seeds; no block finished inside the window, and
   `run_engine.py` writes only after a block returns, so
   `results/engine_v0/F2_2025/` is empty. Every engine cell in the table below is
   therefore PENDING, and the engine numbers quoted in section 3 come from a
   120-simulation development smoke sample, explicitly labelled as such. **Do not
   read section 3 as a gate result.** The block size is the lesson: use
   `--games-per-block 60 --seeds-per-block 5` for a first gradeable pass (about
   300 simulations per block, roughly a minute each), then scale up. RESUME.md
   section 4 has the commands.
3. **No seed-count study has been run** (CLAUDE.md requires one before any ROI or

4. Both engines are graded on the same universe
   (`eval.reference.load_actual_games(2025)`: D-I, non-truncated, completed) by

## 2. Throughput

Measured single-core predict rates on this machine (50,000 rows,
`OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`):

| sub-model | artifact | rows/s/core | calls per possession |
|---|---|---|---|
| clock | `reference_not_adopted_lgbm_quantile` (9 boosters x 300 trees, num_leaves 63) | **4,900** | 1 |
| possession_outcome `first` | `reference_not_adopted_first` (lgbm, 6-class, 400 trees) | 48,000 | ~1 |
| possession_outcome `cont` | `reference_not_adopted_cont` (cascade, 5 logits) | 2,400,000 | ~0.12 |
| fg_make rim | `winner_FGA_rim` (lgbm) | 309,000 | ~0.33 |
| fg_make jump2 | `winner_FGA_jump2` (lgbm) | 407,000 | ~0.23 |
| fg_make FGA_3 | `winner_FGA_3` (A_team ridge) | 5,500,000 | ~0.25 |

**The clock arm is about 85% of the engine's model cost.** Nine LightGBM quantile
regressions, once per possession; everything else together is under a sixth of
it.

End-to-end, single process, `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1`:

| batch | possessions | wall | poss/s/core |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120 sims (40 games x 3 seeds) | 17,406 | 25.2 s | **691** |

691 poss/s/core at a 120-simulation batch is dominated by per-step fixed cost
(about 280 outer steps over only 120 rows). The batch the production runner uses
is 6,250 simulations, where the clock predict dominates and the projected rate is
the clock arm's own 4,900 rows/s/core less the ~20% everything else costs, i.e.
roughly **3,500-4,000 possessions/s/core**.

**Against the target** (5,700 games x 200 seeds in under 2 hours on 20 cores):
the season is 5,710 x 200 x ~145 = **1.66e8 possessions**. At 3,500 poss/s/core
that is 4.7e4 core-seconds = **39 minutes on 20 dedicated cores** -- inside the
target. The caveat is real and was visible all session: this machine was shared
with other model workers (23 python processes at one point), and the realised
aggregate rate was well below the dedicated-core projection. **The target is
projected to be met on dedicated cores and was NOT demonstrated here.**

The stated fallback if it misses is the lookup export. Its cheapest form is
already on disk and wired: the clock bake-off's
`reference_not_adopted_empirical.pkl` IS a lookup table
(`EmpiricalArm.level_pmfs`, a (n_cells, 91) pmf over 7 nested binned state
dimensions of sizes 6 x 5 x 2 x 3 x 3 x 5 x 2), available behind
`ENGINE_CLOCK=reference_empirical`. Binning the quantile arm itself has **not**
been done and its binning error has **not** been measured.

## 3. What the engine actually produced, and the component diagnosis

From the development smoke sample (40 games x 3 seeds, 120 simulations -- small,
and labelled as such; it is a wiring read, not a gate read):

| quantity | engine v0 | actual 2025 | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| total points | 145.68 | 145.5 | +0.2 |
| possessions/game | 72.53 | 67.875 | **+4.65** |
| points per possession | 1.004 | 1.071 | **-0.067** |
| 3PA share of FGA | 0.401 | 0.392 | +0.009 |
| rim share of FGA | 0.349 | -- | -- |
| FTA/FGA | 0.341 | 0.332 | +0.009 |
| TOV per possession | 0.170 | -- | -- |
| OREB% | 0.282 | 0.296 | -0.014 |
| OT rate | 1.7% | 5.58% | **-3.9 pp** |
| minutes per team-game (named players) | 197.9 of 200 | 200 | -2.1 (anonymous tail slots) |
| players with > 0 minutes per team-game (named) | 8.6 | ~9-10 | low |

### Diagnosis, in order of size

**1. The clock model (G1, and through it G2 and G9).** The engine puts 4.65 too
many possessions in a game and scores 6.3% too little on each of them, and the
two almost exactly cancel in the game total (+0.2). That is the offsetting-error
signature CLAUDE.md's multi-level-evidence rule exists to catch: a passing total
over a broken denominator. The component is not in doubt -- the clock bake-off
adopted **nothing** specifically because no arm passed its own emergent G1 gate
(`docs/models/clock/verdict.json`: "no (arm, feature set) combination passed the
pre-registered emergent G1 and PIT gates on F2"), and Decision 7 records this as
the acknowledged risk of making pace emergent: *"G1 now depends on the
clock-consumption model being right by state."* The engine inherits that failure
exactly as predicted. Round 2 of the clock bake-off was still running while this
engine was built.

Note what this is *not*: it is not a points-per-possession defect in the shot
models. Shot mix lands within 1 pp of actual on all three legs (3PA share, FTA
rate, rim share), which is the possession-outcome model doing its job. The PPP
miss is the denominator.

**2. The overtime rate (G7).** 1.7% against 5.58%. The engine reaches the end of
regulation tied far too rarely. That is a **dispersion** symptom in the late
second half, not an overtime-rule symptom -- the OT rules themselves are
implemented and tested (5-minute periods, fresh jump ball, team fouls carried
over as an extension of the second half, repeat until untied, and
`tests/test_engine.py` asserts no game ends tied). Control fails the same gate
from the opposite direction (1.83%) with a completely different mechanism (a
Gaussian margin twice too wide, yet too few exact ties), which says the shared
cause is that neither engine reproduces the *discreteness* of a basketball
margin near zero. For the possession engine the suspects are the clock model's
late-period duration distribution and the engine's end-of-period censoring read
(model.md section 2.2), in that order.

**3. The team-foul / personal-foul split.** Team fouls (which drive the bonus)
come from the foul events the L3 vocabulary can express plus a measured
non-shooting-foul accrual rate of 0.12335 per possession; personal fouls (which
drive foul-outs and therefore minutes) come from `rotation`'s fitted `fpm`
hazard. The two are **not reconciled**, and the engine says so
(`provisional_foul_accrual`). FTA/FGA landing +0.009 high suggests the accrual
rate is slightly hot, but that cannot be separated from the possession-count
error until the clock is fixed: a rate per possession applied to 4.65 extra
possessions produces extra fouls mechanically.

**4. FGA_3 and Decision 8.** `winner_FGA_3.joblib` predates Decision 8 and holds
`team_baseline`, whose shooter slope ratio is 0.0086 -- flat in shooter skill,
which Decision 8 calls out explicitly. The engine defaults to
`ENGINE_FG3=decision8` and runs its own LightGBM refit for that class, recorded
in `run_meta.json`. This is a sub-model export defect, not an engine defect, and
it is the one place the engine deliberately does not use an on-disk "winner".

**5. The player layer (G8).** 8.6 named players with minutes per team-game
against roughly 9-10 real, and 197.9 of 200 team minutes reaching named players.
Both have the same cause: 10.0% of team-games have no usable rotation prior (a
team's first game of the season) and fall back to anonymous fitted tail slots,
which carry minutes but no athlete identity and are excluded from
`players.parquet`. Separately, L19's open defect (every allocator
under-concentrates the top of the lineup) is inherited unchanged and will show in
G8's top-1 FGA share.

## 5. The honest bottom line

ENGINE v0 is wired end to end and produces contract-valid output with a real
player layer, which Control cannot. It does **not** yet match Control on the
game-level gates, and the reason is a single identified component rather than a
diffuse inaccuracy: the clock model, whose own bake-off adopted nothing on the
gate the engine now fails. Per the standing rule "never accept a downstream stage
that compensates for a known upstream bias", no adjustment was applied anywhere,
and none should be until the clock round-2 winner is wired and G1 is re-read.
